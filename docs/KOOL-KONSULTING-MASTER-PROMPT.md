# Kool Konsulting — complete brand and website master prompt

Prepared 5 October 2026 from the live-site audit, the owner's answers, reference-site inspection and provider-published pricing research.

This is a brief for the future redesign. Creating this document does not change or publish the website. Copy everything between **MASTER PROMPT START** and **MASTER PROMPT END** into the design/build agent. The research notes after the prompt explain the recommendations and their limits.

---

**MASTER PROMPT START**

You are acting as a senior brand strategist, creative director, product designer, motion designer and frontend engineer. Completely reimagine the identity and website of **Kool Konsulting**. Produce a coherent brand and an implemented, polished website with meaningful interactive demonstrations.

The current website is https://kool-konsulting.vercel.app/. Its visual identity was an experiment. Treat this as a full creative reset. The existing layout, colours, logo, typefaces, ledger metaphor and component styling are all replaceable. Preserve verified business facts and useful technical functionality, not the old aesthetic.

## 1. The business and the decisions already made

Kool Konsulting is a founder-led technology studio based in Nagpur, India. Kulvir Sharma is the founder. Its priority audiences are:

1. Ambitious startups building or improving digital products and internal operations.
2. Premium creative and lifestyle brands seeking distinctive digital experiences and more connected business processes.
3. Luxury restaurants, boutique hotels and hospitality businesses seeking refined guest experiences and better operational coordination.

Its leading services are **AI and automation**, **websites**, and **apps**. Custom business workflows belong within these offers rather than being presented as an unrelated fourth business.

The primary conversion is **Book a call**. The main design direction is **balanced premium technology** with an immersive, concept-driven experience throughout. All branding can be redesigned. Keep the business name Kool Konsulting; develop a new visual identity around it.

The owner wants technology that feels luxurious in its craft and useful in its effect on a business. Express that through original art direction, thoughtful interactions, dependable systems and personal service. Avoid making vague claims about luxury the substitute for showing capability.

Do not reopen these settled strategic questions. Make reasonable creative and implementation choices within this brief. Identify external configuration or factual gaps precisely when they become necessary.

## 2. Positioning and verbal identity

Position the business as:

**A technology studio creating beautiful digital experiences and intelligent operations for ambitious brands.**

The connection between customer experience and operational intelligence is the organising idea. A beautiful restaurant website should lead into a useful reservation journey. A lifestyle storefront should connect to an organised clienteling workflow. A startup app should connect to onboarding and team operations.

Use this initial headline and brand promise:

**Beautiful experiences. Intelligent operations.**

Supporting hero copy:

“We design websites, build apps and connect AI workflows for ambitious startups, lifestyle brands and hospitality businesses.”

Primary CTA: **Book a call**.

Secondary CTA: **Explore the demos** until approved client work is ready.

A small descriptor can say “An independent technology studio, based in Nagpur.” Do not imply global offices, a large team or established luxury clients without evidence.

The voice is precise, intelligent, warm and quietly confident. “Kool” provides room for wit in small moments; write clear professional English for service descriptions and commercial details. Use Indian English consistently. Avoid excessive puns, aggressive sales language, invented urgency, “revolutionary” promises and competitor put-downs. Prefer concrete explanations such as “An enquiry becomes a qualified lead” and “Your team receives the booking details in one place.”

Retire the existing emphasis on registers, mandis and manual bookkeeping from the core brand story. Genuine projects from those sectors can remain in the portfolio when relevant and substantiated.

## 3. Creative concept: the folded surface and the connected system

Use a distinctive geometric folded plane as the recurring visual motif. Its internal working name is **the Kool Fold**. It must have recognisable geometry derived from a custom K mark.

The front of the plane represents the polished experience customers see. As it unfolds, it reveals the organised connections and decisions that make the experience work. This single idea should connect the logo, hero, service transitions, demonstrations, diagrams and closing scene.

Make the object feel crafted: porcelain or satin metal surfaces, controlled reflections, a precise angled seam, deep ink edges and restrained electric colour. Give it convincing depth and lighting. It should remain recognisable in a flat silhouette.

Use one consistent visual world. A page full of unrelated floating objects, glowing globes, glass cards, stock robots and particles would dilute the concept. The visual must explain a relationship or reveal an experience.

The site should feel immersive through continuity, pacing, scale, material and interaction. It needs still moments where visitors can read, compare and act.

## 5. Complete brand system

### Logo and wordmark

Create a custom wordmark for **Kool Konsulting** and a simple supporting K symbol built around the fold geometry. Make the full name legible in the website header and ordinary business use. A more expressive treatment of “Kool” may appear in editorial compositions, but the identity must not become ambiguous.

Supply horizontal and stacked lockups, symbol-only, monochrome, reversed and small-size versions. Test the symbol at 16, 24 and 32 pixels and the full lockup on mobile. Optical spacing matters. The identity must work without gradients, transparency or animation.

Create the final logo as editable vector geometry. Do not use generated raster text as the production wordmark. Do not claim trademark clearance without doing that work.

### Palette

Use these as a concrete starting system, refining only when visual and contrast checks justify it:

| Role | Starting colour | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Porcelain | `#F3F2EE` | Main light sections, warm neutral canvas |
| White | `#FFFFFF` | Functional surfaces and selected content |
| Ink | `#0E1016` | Primary text and cinematic dark sections |
| Graphite | `#1A1E29` | Layers within dark compositions |
| Electric violet | `#5145E5` | Main brand accent and selected actions |
| Ice blue | `#7DDCF2` | Light, signal paths and limited highlights |
| Brushed stone | `#B8B3A9` | Material imagery and secondary decoration |

Treat these as roles, not a requirement to use every colour in each section. Begin with a roughly balanced light/dark journey, giving explanatory and commercial content comfortable reading surfaces. Use contrast-verified foreground/background pairs. Ice blue and stone are not default small-text colours on white.

WhatsApp green belongs to a secondary WhatsApp control or logo. The main booking CTA belongs to the Kool brand system.

### Typography and layout

Use a clean variable sans serif with enough personality for large editorial typography. **Manrope** is the initial implementation choice, subject to checking the actual font files and licence. Use **IBM Plex Mono** sparingly for short workflow annotations. Design the wordmark separately.

Desktop hero type should generally sit around 76–104px where the content fits. Mobile hero type should generally sit around 40–54px with deliberate line breaks. Use fluid sizing, and adjust to content rather than clipping text to meet a number. Body copy should generally be 18–20px on desktop and 16–18px on mobile, with comfortable line spacing. Important information should not be pushed into 10–12px labels.

Use a disciplined spacing scale and a responsive grid. Mix generous editorial layouts, full-width visual moments, precise diagrams and functional interface surfaces. Avoid repeating the same rounded card treatment in every section. Use whitespace to establish relationships, rather than padding every component excessively.

Use consistent icon stroke weights, subtle borders and restrained corner radii. Reserve pronounced dimensional lighting for the concept scenes.

### Brand outputs

Deliver an editable logo set, favicon family, social avatar, social sharing image template, colour/type/spacing tokens, icon guidance and a concise brand guide. Include example applications for a proposal cover, a social post and a digital business-card layout. These should demonstrate one consistent system, not become unrelated design projects.

## 6. Information architecture

Use a focused top navigation: **Expertise**, **Lab**, **Studio**, and **Book a call**. A Work destination can be introduced when genuinely presentable client work is available. Keep investment information easy to reach from services and the footer.

Build these destinations:

- Home: the complete brand story and primary conversion journey.
- Expertise/services overview: three clear service families.
- AI & Automation: useful workflows, evaluation, human handoff and integration boundaries.
- Websites & Experiences: brand websites, commerce or hospitality journeys, CMS and performance.
- Apps & Digital Products: web apps, mobile apps, MVPs and connected customer/staff journeys.
- Lab: polished, clearly labelled concept demonstrations.
- Work/case-study template: ready for verified project material, with distinct concept and client-work states.
- Studio: founder, approach, verified background and delivery model.
- Investment: scoped indicative ranges and engagement information.
- Book a call/contact: real scheduling when configured, with an honest fallback.
- Privacy, terms, accessible 404 and confirmation states.

Inspect the existing routes before changing them. Preserve useful existing URLs where possible. Add redirects only when destinations are genuinely equivalent. Keep verified project information available through a suitable design instead of deleting it simply because the homepage's audience has changed. Do not silently relabel old client projects as new premium work.

## 7. Homepage storyboard

### A. Arrival: the complete promise

Show the logo, clear navigation, headline, supporting sentence and booking CTA immediately. The visitor must understand the offer before any animation finishes.

Give the folded form a substantial, integrated place in the hero composition. It should not look like an unrelated widget inside a rounded card. It begins in a crafted, readable state, then gently opens into three purposeful layers: a customer-facing experience, a decision/workflow layer and a useful operational outcome.

A hotel enquiry, a lifestyle product interaction and a startup onboarding screen can appear as short selectable examples. Keep the labels in HTML and the business relationship easy to understand. The scene must never be the sole source of essential information.

### B. Three worlds of application

Introduce **Startups**, **Lifestyle**, and **Hospitality** through three art-directed compositions. Use an interactive selector with a good initial state and keyboard/touch equivalents. Each audience selection changes the example interface and the explanation.

- Startups: onboarding, product activation and organised lead routing.
- Lifestyle: product discovery, appointments, clienteling and enquiry follow-up.
- Hospitality: guest enquiries, dining or stay preferences, reservation requests and staff coordination.

Write “Designed for” or describe these as potential applications. Do not imply these are existing client relationships.

### C. Expertise, demonstrated

Show the three service families in commercial priority order: AI & Automation, Websites & Experiences, Apps & Digital Products.

Each needs one short outcome-led heading, a concise scope, a useful visual, a relevant example and a route to more detail. Use a distinct composition for each, unified by the fold and connection motif. Avoid long capability inventories on the homepage.

### D. The interactive lab

Show three original demonstrations specified below. Feature one strongly and give the others clear entry points, rather than loading three large simulations at once. Provide a visible sample-data label before interaction.

The section should help a visitor imagine a useful project and lead naturally to “Discuss a similar project” or “Book a call.”

### E. How we work and who is accountable

Combine a clear four-stage process with a concise founder introduction:

1. Understand the business and define the opportunity.
2. Design the experience and prove the critical workflow.
3. Build, test and refine with regular demonstrations.
4. Launch, hand over and support under an agreed scope.

Use verified founder material. Emphasise direct involvement and commercial understanding. If an approved photograph is unavailable, use a designed typographic founder panel and an internal asset placeholder. Do not generate a fictitious founder portrait or invent a team.

### F. Investment preview and essential questions

Provide a compact indication of engagement sizes and link to the full investment page. Pricing should help qualify the call without turning the page into a complex calculator. Answer the main questions about scope, timeline, ownership, integrations and support.

### G. A memorable ending with a useful action

The connected surfaces resolve into the K symbol. Pair this with a clear invitation:

“Let's make your next move work beautifully.”

CTA: **Book a call**.

State what the call is for: discussing the opportunity, identifying an appropriate first scope and assessing fit. Use confirmed duration/availability if configured; do not invent real appointment slots.

Keep the complete homepage edited. Avoid repeating the founder promise, every industry, all process details, a competitor table and a full estimator in separate lengthy sections.

## 8. Demonstrations and placeholder policy

Create polished fictional scenarios under **Kool Lab**. Every demo must be labelled **Concept demo · Sample data** at entry, including its preview card and detail page. Describe what it demonstrates, not fabricated business results.

### Demo 1: Hospitality concierge

Create a tasteful guest-facing interface connected to a clear staff-side view. Offer hotel and dining scenarios if both can be implemented to the same standard.

Let the user choose meaningful details such as dates, party size, preferred time or stay preferences from a controlled sample dataset. Show the resulting request, information lookup, availability response and staff handoff. Include an unavailable option and a useful alternative. When the visitor asks for something outside the sample knowledge, offer human follow-up instead of making up an answer.

All selected values must carry through accurately. End with “Demo complete — no reservation has been made.” Do not send messages, create reservations or trigger payments.

### Demo 2: Lifestyle discovery and clienteling

Create an editorial product-discovery interface using original or appropriately licensed sample imagery. Let the user choose an occasion, preference or product attribute. Show a coherent recommendation, product detail and appointment/enquiry summary connected to a sample client record.

Use a clearly fictional sample collection without implying that it is a real client. Keep simulated inventory, selected quantity and recommendations as distinct state. Explain a recommendation briefly. A changed input should produce a meaningful changed result.

### Demo 3: Startup onboarding and workflow

Show a potential customer moving from signup or enquiry through a configurable qualification rule to a sample team view. Allow a visitor to change one rule or requirement and see the resulting route. Include a human-review branch so the demonstration communicates control as well as automation.

Do not decorate the interface with made-up revenue, conversion gains or live-user counts. A small labelled activity history showing the visitor's own simulated actions is sufficient.

### Shared interaction requirements

Use deterministic local simulations initially. A real LLM connection is not necessary to demonstrate interaction quality and must not introduce unapproved usage costs. Do not describe scripted demonstrations as live AI.

Every control must work. Include initial, changed, loading where meaningful, empty, error, completion and reset states. Keep tab selection and result announcements accessible. Avoid fake progress delays; any short illustrative sequence should be skippable and replayable.

Design real reusable templates for screenshots, recordings, testimonials and outcomes. In internal previews, missing assets may have subtle named placeholders. In the public experience, omit unfilled proof sections or replace them with explicitly labelled concept work. Do not expose giant blank holes, lorem ipsum, fabricated logos, quotations, review stars or unsourced performance claims.

Store content type and readiness explicitly so future client work can replace a concept without rewriting the layout. A concept must never inherit a client-work badge or factual result schema automatically.

## 9. Motion and 3D specification

Create a small family of connected motion behaviours:

- The fold opens to reveal a system or experience.
- A restrained line traces the flow from input to decision to outcome.
- Interfaces assemble or change in response to a meaningful selection.
- Project imagery reveals through an original mask derived from the fold geometry.
- Buttons and links respond with precise, subtle motion.
- The ending resolves the form into the brand mark.

Use short interface transitions, generally 160–280ms, and more deliberate scene changes around 500–900ms when the content warrants them. These are starting ranges; tune the result visually. Avoid endless staggering that makes readers wait for sentences.

Desktop scroll-linked scenes can be cinematic, but they must finish while their content is visible. Keep ordinary vertical scrolling, browser history and keyboard navigation intact. Any sticky storytelling section must be short, clearly escapable and unnecessary for reaching the booking action.

On mobile, use a carefully composed equivalent with shorter transitions or user-triggered scene changes. Do not shrink a desktop dashboard until its labels become unreadable. Make meaningful information available outside the illustration.

Do not replace the cursor, hijack scroll, force horizontal navigation, autoplay sound or require drag gestures to access information. Optional pointer parallax must be small and irrelevant to task completion. Provide a pause/control mechanism for persistent nonessential motion, and honour reduced-motion preferences from the first render.

Use at most one actively rendering WebGL scene at a time. Defer heavy scenes until needed, stop offscreen and background-tab rendering, cap rendering resolution sensibly, and dispose of unused resources. Render text and functional controls in semantic HTML.

Prepare an art-directed static composition for every important scene. The hero fallback must actually depict the concept, not repeat the logo on an empty background. Keep it visible until a meaningful 3D frame is ready. Handle slow assets, failed models and WebGL context loss without a blank section. Reduced-motion users should receive a complete, attractive composition.

## 10. Call booking and lead experience

Use one consistent primary CTA: **Book a call**. Keep it visible in the desktop header and reachable on mobile without covering content or form controls. WhatsApp is a secondary contact option.

Build a dedicated booking page that explains the purpose of the conversation. When the owner provides an actual scheduling URL/account, integrate it with a clear timezone display, available slots, rescheduling information and a reliable confirmation state. Lazy-load a heavy scheduling embed or offer a direct link if that provides a better experience.

Capture only useful qualification information: name, work email or preferred contact, business/website, project interest, and a short description. Budget range and timing can be optional, including a “Not sure yet” choice. Keep the flow brief.

Preserve the visitor's service or demo context when they enter booking. Confirm an appointment only after a real provider success event. A click or an iframe opening is not a completed booking.

If scheduling is not configured, show an honest **Request a call** form with the existing functioning enquiry infrastructure where appropriate. A successful request means a request was received, not that a calendar appointment exists. If delivery is unavailable, show a usable contact fallback and an accurate error; never simulate success.

Use configuration fields for the real booking URL, contact email and provider settings. Do not invent contact details, accounts or availability. Keep the existing verified phone/WhatsApp details unless the owner changes them.

## 11. Pricing and commercial presentation

The following are recommended starting assumptions for this redesign, informed by public local and regional comparables. They are not a statistical Nagpur average or approved final commercial quotes. Keep them in one editable source of truth and identify them as indicative project ranges.

| Engagement | Proposed typical investment | Scope boundary |
|---|---:|---|
| Focused automation sprint | ₹30,000–₹60,000 | One bounded workflow, a limited number of integrations, testing and handover |
| AI assistant or connected workflow | ₹75,000–₹2,00,000 | Defined knowledge source, integrations, evaluation, logs and human handoff |
| Premium brand website | ₹60,000–₹1,50,000 | Custom art direction, core pages, CMS, considered motion and conversion/booking integration |
| Immersive flagship website | ₹1,50,000–₹3,00,000+ | Original visual direction, substantial motion/3D and a specifically agreed asset-production scope |
| Focused web-app MVP | ₹1,50,000–₹3,50,000 | One central workflow with bounded roles, backend and integrations |
| Cross-platform mobile MVP | ₹2,00,000–₹5,00,000 | Defined screens/features, backend/admin scope and store-submission work |
| Website care | ₹3,000–₹8,000/month | Defined monitoring, updates, backups and content-change allowance |
| App or AI system care | ₹10,000–₹25,000/month | Agreed monitoring, evaluation, incident response and support-hour allowance |

Complex platforms, substantial native builds and unusual integrations require a scoped proposal. These tiers are alternatives with different scopes; do not present overlapping inclusions as compulsory paid extras.

On the homepage, summarise three entry routes: automation from ₹30,000, websites from ₹60,000 and focused web apps from ₹1,50,000, only while clearly preserving the associated scope. Give more detail on the investment page. The main CTA remains Book a call.

State that final fees follow discovery and a written scope. Separate build cost, third-party subscriptions/usage, hosting, content production, optional ongoing support and applicable taxes. Confirm the business's actual tax treatment rather than copying a blanket GST statement without checking it.

Flag original 3D production, photography, video, brand identity work, content migration and bespoke integrations as specifically scoped items. Do not imply that every listed service is included at the lowest price. Do not promise unlimited revisions, unlimited maintenance or round-the-clock founder availability.

Before these become final published commitments, validate them against actual delivery effort, specialist costs, support capacity and margin. Keep this validation as a launch dependency, not an excuse to leave the design incomplete.

## 12. Engineering and content architecture

Inspect the repository and follow the actual current application. At audit time it used Next.js App Router, React, TypeScript, Tailwind, Framer Motion and React Three Fiber/Three.js. The README contained older Astro information, so it is not a reliable description of the active implementation. Re-check package files before changing anything.

Keep the stable existing stack unless a specific requirement justifies a change. This brand reset does not require a framework migration. Choose a clear owner for each animation; do not have multiple libraries control the same transform or scroll state.

Use server-rendered semantic content and isolate interactive client components. Centralise services, prices, contact settings, concept data, real case studies and asset metadata. Keep copy editable without modifying animation logic.

Preserve and improve useful enquiry validation, spam protection and error handling. Keep secrets on the server. Use explicit configuration for optional integrations. Document external dependencies and missing credentials without exposing them.

Keep approved factual content separate from presentation placeholders. Provide a content/asset register with each item's source, permission/licence, status and intended slot. Use generated imagery only for suitable illustrative concepts, labelled where necessary; never use it to fabricate evidence of actual client work.

Build a coherent case-study template: context, problem, role, scope, visual demonstration, implementation explanation, verified outcomes when available and a relevant CTA. Quotation marks and testimonial styling require an actual approved quote.

## 13. Accessibility, performance and search

Target WCAG 2.2 AA. Include keyboard-complete navigation, visible focus, a skip link, sensible landmarks and headings, clear labels, contrast-checked states and meaningful image alternatives. Mobile navigation needs appropriate expanded state, Escape behaviour, focus management/restoration and background interaction handling.

Provide semantic selected/pressed states for selectors and tabs. Announce meaningful results politely without announcing animation frames. Keep focus unobscured by sticky elements. Support zoom and touch without overlap; aim for approximately 44px effective targets for primary touch controls.

Performance targets are LCP at or below 2.5 seconds, INP at or below 200ms and CLS at or below 0.1 at the 75th percentile in field data when available. These are goals, not claims about a site that has not yet been measured. Use lab checks during development, and distinguish them from real-user results.

Make content and booking usable before heavy visual assets arrive. Start with an initial compressed-resource budget of roughly 1.5MB excluding deferred 3D/demo assets; profile and explain material exceptions. Optimise images and fonts, reserve layout dimensions, avoid eagerly loading below-fold video, and keep offscreen scenes idle. Never claim an accessibility score alone proves accessibility or a desktop test proves mobile performance.

Create unique titles and descriptions, proper canonical URLs, social previews, sitemap/robots configuration and appropriate structured data using verified facts. Fix the existing duplicated company-name suffix in page titles. Do not publish review/rating schema for nonexistent reviews. Retain relevant Nagpur context while writing for the broader chosen audiences; avoid repetitive location-keyword copy.

Use the actual production domain when configured and handle redirects properly if the domain changes. Keep internal unfinished previews out of the public search index without indiscriminately hiding the finished Lab pages.

When an analytics provider is configured and appropriate consent choices are implemented, measure CTA entry points, booking starts, confirmed bookings, submitted call requests and useful demo completions. Do not treat a booking click as a booking, or send raw enquiry text/contact information into general analytics events.

## 14. Delivery sequence

Work through these phases, keeping each output concrete and reviewable:

1. Inspect the current implementation and verified business content; record reusable functionality and content gaps.
2. Develop the new wordmark/symbol, tokens, typography, motion grammar and a concise visual direction board. Present one coherent preferred direction with rationale.
3. Build the header, hero and one complete interactive demo as a high-fidelity slice. Check it on desktop and mobile before duplicating patterns.
4. Implement the complete homepage, inner-page templates, Lab and booking/request flow.
5. Add the remaining demonstrations, responsive art direction, fallbacks and final content organisation.
6. Perform functional, visual, accessibility and performance checks; resolve defects and document remaining external setup needs.
7. Deliver the working local/preview result, reusable brand assets, concise brand guide, configuration notes and a factual verification report.

Continue autonomously with reversible design and implementation work. Ask only for information that truly blocks an integration or a factual publication decision. Do not purchase assets, create paid services, change external accounts or deploy to production merely because this prompt describes the final website; those actions need the user's applicable authorisation.

## 15. Acceptance criteria

The result is complete only when:

- A new visitor can identify the offer, intended audience and primary action within the first screen.
- The logo, site, motion and brand applications clearly belong to one original identity.
- The site feels materially more expressive and technically accomplished than the current version, with a consistent concept beyond the hero.
- Every major visual supports a story or useful interaction. The page also has calm, readable sections.
- All three audience groups see a relevant application, with no unsupported claim that they are existing clients.
- Every demo control changes the correct state; alternatives, reset, errors and completion work. A chosen time or quantity never changes silently.
- Concept/sample labels are visible before interaction, and no demo performs a real transaction or implies a real reservation.
- Real booking and call-request flows have distinct truthful outcomes, and unavailable integrations are reported accurately.
- Client proof placeholders can be replaced cleanly, without publishing fabricated evidence or obvious empty sections.
- Essential journeys work at 360, 390, 768, 1024, 1440 and 1920px widths, with 200% zoom, keyboard input, touch and reduced motion.
- The hero and every important scene remain useful with WebGL unavailable, model loading failed or motion disabled.
- The mobile menu, focus order, selected states, form errors and sticky controls are usable.
- No unexpected horizontal page overflow, clipped headings, broken links, inaccessible hover-only actions, hydration errors or relevant console errors remain.
- Core content and primary actions remain available without waiting for 3D. Performance results are measured and reported with the conditions used.
- Pricing, contact details, privacy content, service inclusions and project status come from editable, consistent data.
- The delivery report lists changed files/assets, what was actually tested and any remaining owner-supplied configuration. Never mark an untested integration as verified.

The final experience should make a prospective client think: **“They understand the standard of experience I want my customers to have—and they can build the systems behind it.”**

## Research notes and limits

Research date: **5 October 2026**. Recommendations above combine the owner's decisions with design judgement. They are not a claim that one visual style or provider is objectively the best.

### Design reference evidence

Lusion, 14islands and Linear were inspected visually in the browser as well as through their published pages. The useful principles were dimensional craft, visually strong project presentation and precise interface storytelling. Their speed and animation performance across devices were not benchmarked.
